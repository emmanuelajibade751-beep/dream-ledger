Five dreams, one household that never gets to dream, and one paid in dollars — ~82,000 synthetic transactions over 6–10 years each. Every line below is a life being paid for: cement, tuition, visa fees, generator fuel, bets. Click a person to enter their life; hover the dots to see the moment a milestone landed — then scroll down and shock the city.

The climb — what each dream accumulated

Journeys aligned at month 0. One of these lines rockets — click it to find out why.

How to read this

Each line is one person's cumulative dream spending — money that physically became the dream (cement, tuition, tickets, content). Steeper is not always better: a line can rise because the dream is succeeding, or because the world made the same dream more expensive. The dots are real milestones from each ledger.

⚠ Honesty note: each person's “% of target” is measured against a different kind of target, so percentages are never compared between people here — each life is read on its own terms. All data is synthetic (real 2025 anchor prices, interpolated daily entries) and illustrative, not predictive.

How to read this

Each row of squares is one year; each square one week. Colour answers a single question: what did the spare money do that week — build the dream (green) or leak away (red)? Survival spending is deliberately ignored — it dominates every week of every life, and would paint the whole wall grey. The Landlord greens up in his build years; AMA is a near-solid red wall — a betting habit visible from orbit.

⚠ Colour shows direction, not size: a ₦500 green week and a ₦500,000 green week look the same. Hover for the actual amounts.

How to read this

“The dream (real terms)” counts what the spending could actually buy — so when a currency collapse doubles the price of a foreign-priced dream, the same naira builds half as much. Dreams also pause automatically when a household is drowning (deep in debt, dream spending stops) — watch lines go flat, not just lower. Money already turned into cement or a skill doesn't un-become the dream.

⚠ This is an illustration engine, not a forecast: multipliers are curated judgments applied to synthetic ledgers. It shows how exposure differs — who bends, who breaks, who benefits — not what will happen.

How to read this

Block height = naira actually built into the dream, on one city-wide scale (the Landlord's finished house ≈ full height — so AMA's ₦700k hustle stays low and the ₦66m degree towers). Colour is what this month is doing: green = building, gold = arrived (the dream completed), red = leaking more than building, grey = just surviving, dark = the dream has died (paused so long it never restarts). The flat district on the far left is the Control — households with no dream; shocks can only push them deeper. Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, hover a block for its story.

⚠ The 1,000 households are procedural variations of the seven synthetic ledgers — an illustration of differing exposure, not a demographic model of Lagos.

What the money became — the dream as objects

Everything the ledger literally bought, tiled by cost. The red tiles sit where they sat in the life: right among the dream.

How to read this

Tile area = naira spent. This is the Peter Menzel “person with all their possessions” view of a dream: the house appears in parts, the degree in instalments — and the leak appears as red objects sitting among them, bought with the same hands.
